# Purpose

This document defines the standard policy for primary keys, unique identifiers, and business identifiers used throughout the LOUTAS Care platform.

The objective is to ensure consistency, uniqueness, interoperability, and long-term scalability across all modules.

This policy applies to every business entity defined within the platform.

---

# Scope

This policy applies to:

- Business entities
- Master data
- Transactional data
- Clinical records
- Billing records
- Administrative records
- Security-related entities
- Future modules

---

# Identifier Categories

The platform distinguishes between two categories of identifiers.

## System Identifier

A system identifier uniquely identifies a database record.

Characteristics:

- Generated by the system.
- Never changes.
- Not meaningful to end users.
- Used internally for relationships.

Examples:

- Patient.Id
- Appointment.Id
- Visit.Id
- Invoice.Id

---

## Business Identifier

A business identifier is intended for operational use by users.

Characteristics:

- Human-readable where applicable.
- May follow business numbering rules.
- May appear in printed documents or user interfaces.
- Must remain unique within its business scope.

Examples include:

- Patient Number
- Invoice Number
- Visit Number
- Appointment Number

---

# Primary Key Policy

Each business table shall contain exactly one primary key.

Primary keys shall:

- Be immutable.
- Be unique.
- Never be reused.
- Never encode business meaning.
- Exist for the lifetime of the record.

---

# Foreign Key Policy

Foreign keys shall reference the primary key of the related entity.

Relationships shall never depend on business identifiers.

Example:

Correct

Appointment.PatientId → Patient.Id

Avoid

Appointment.PatientNumber → Patient.PatientNumber

---

# Business Numbering

Business numbering policies shall be managed independently from database primary keys.

Business identifiers may:

- Include prefixes.
- Follow configurable numbering formats.
- Be reset according to approved business rules where applicable.

Business numbering shall never replace system identifiers.

---

# Identifier Immutability

Primary keys shall never be modified after record creation.

Business identifiers should only change where explicitly supported by approved business rules.

---

# Uniqueness

Every primary key must be globally unique within its entity.

Business identifiers must remain unique within their defined business context.

---

# User Visibility

System identifiers are intended for internal processing.

Business identifiers are intended for operational users.

Applications should display business identifiers whenever appropriate.

---

# External Integration

External systems should integrate using approved identifiers.

Where business identifiers are exchanged, their uniqueness and lifecycle must be documented by the owning module.

---

# Security Considerations

Primary keys shall not expose business-sensitive information.

Identifiers should not reveal:

- Business volume
- Patient information
- Financial information
- Internal sequencing logic

unless explicitly required by approved business processes.

---

# Audit Requirements

Audit records shall preserve both:

- System identifier
- Business identifier (where applicable)

to support traceability and historical analysis.

---

# Future Compatibility

New modules introduced into the platform shall comply with this identifier policy unless an approved architectural decision specifies otherwise.
